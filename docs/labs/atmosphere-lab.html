00

Two things to settle before anything gets built

Neither is an aesthetic question, but both gate the aesthetic work — and one of them is the reason a lab comes before a repo change.

Architecture

There is no theme mechanism yet

Today :root is the dark theme. The .light class exists in index.css but nothing ever applies it — the design doc says to treat light mode as unsupported until you decide otherwise.

Multiple themes each with variations needs three things that don't exist: token sets keyed by theme rather than baked into :root, a provider plus persistence, and — for the world themes below — a per-theme graphics layer that the terminal themes never load.

Recommendation: explore in a lab first, exactly as Card Skin and Keycap did. Build the mechanism once you know how many themes are real and whether any of them need more than tokens.

Dependencies

Three.js wouldn't conflict — weight is the real cost

You already ship motion@13.1.0; there is no three in your 25 dependencies. They don't collide: Motion animates DOM and SVG, Three renders into its own <canvas>. Different layers.

The actual cost is bundle size and contention. Three is larger than most of your current dependency set combined, and this app is a virtualized episode list with pixel-snapped hit-testing — a WebGL layer competing for the same frame is a real risk to the thing that has to stay smooth.

Recommendation: if a world theme earns it, WebGL as atmosphere only — landing hero, depth wash behind the catalog — never behind the instrument, and lazy-loaded so terminal users pay nothing. I couldn't verify ThreeUI's licensing or MCP offering; that page renders client-side and returned nothing fetchable.

01

Terminal lineage

Variations that keep the mono / near-sharp / dash-bordered vocabulary intact. All three are token swaps — cheap to try, cheap to abandon, and none of them threaten the rulings already locked into instrument_v1.

phosphor_v1amber CRT

ep:1ep:46

Phosphor

One hue, five intensities, a faint bloom.

The hook: forces the strip to encode type by intensity and hatch instead of hue — which is the accessibility win hiding inside an aesthetic. If five episode types stay legible on a single amber, they stay legible for every colour-vision profile you'd otherwise have to validate.

The risk: that's also the hard part. Five steps on one hue is a narrow ladder, and your --type-* set currently earns ΔE 9.6 across ten pairs. This one has to prove it.

token swaphatch carries type

blueprint_v1drafting

ep:1ep:46

Blueprint

Cyan on Prussian, dimension lines, callout leaders.

The hook: the app is a measuring instrument and this is the native visual language for measurement. "Episode 337 corresponds to chapter 291" is a dimension line — a drafting convention that already exists for stating a correspondence between two points, with the value written on the rule.

Un-adapted manga gets the draughtsman's own honesty device: a break symbol and NOT TO SCALE, rather than a span drawn as though it were measured.

tokens + line-workstrip → measured rule

daylight_v1light terminal

ep:1ep:46

Daylight

The same terminal, in a room with a window.

The hook: the least exciting specimen here and probably the most used. It closes the open decision in the design doc and makes the app legible on a laptop outdoors — the exact situation where someone finishes an episode and wants to know where to start reading.

The trap: don't invert the dark tokens. Dark grounds hide a lot of contrast sin; a light terminal needs its type palette re-derived from scratch and re-validated, not flipped.

token swapre-derive, never invert
02

Depth worlds

Your abyss direction, taken three ways. All three exploit the same structural fact: your navigation already has depth — landing, then catalog, then the card opens, then the episode list — and a series' chapter count is itself a distance downward.

abyss_v1layer 4

ep:1ep:46

The Abyss

Strata. Light attenuates as you descend.

The hook: depth is the navigation. The landing sits at the surface — verdant, hazy, overexposed; the catalog is the second layer; opening a card drops you to the fourth, where the ground darkens, particulate drifts, and the only colour left is bioluminescent. Each layer is a complete token set, and moving between them is a transition rather than a page.

The detail worth stealing: the curse of ascending. Going back up costs something — a held beat, a bleach of colour — so the descent feels one-way in the way the source material means it.

graphics layernav depth = token set

sounding_v1survey chart

ep:1ep:46

Sounding

Bathymetric survey — contours, depth figures, chart paper.

The hook: the instrument version of the abyss, and the strongest fit for your product promise. Nautical charts have a formal convention for water that has not been surveyed: it is left blank. Not estimated, not interpolated from the depths around it — blank, because a guessed depth wrecks ships.

That is // no_source and the honest dash, already solved by a discipline that has taken it seriously for two centuries. The strip becomes a depth trace; the manga tail is unsounded water.

tokens + line-workblank = unsurveyed

core_v1stratigraphic

ep:1ep:46

Core Sample

A drilled column, logged by hand, read top to bottom.

The hook: your strip rotated ninety degrees already is a core log. Canon is bedrock, filler is intrusion, volume boundaries are bedding planes, and the manga-only tail is the section below where the drill stopped.

The cheapest structural idea on this board: it needs earth tones and a rotation, not a graphics layer — and it turns the vertical episode list and the horizontal strip into the same object seen twice.

token swapstrip = the list, rotated
03

Archive & document

Three paper worlds. They read closest together in swatch form because their separation is typographic and structural rather than chromatic — judge these on their type and their furniture, not their palettes.

concordance_v1vellum

ep:1ep:46

Concordance

A critical edition — parallel columns, marginal glosses, rubrication.

The hook: the app is literally a concordance, and textual scholarship has spent centuries building display conventions for exactly your problem — two versions of one text, set in parallel, with every divergence annotated in the margin rather than smoothed away.

It also hands you the right word. A gap in a manuscript is a lacuna: marked, measured, never invented. Filler becomes scribal interpolation — present in one witness, absent in the other, and labelled as such.

tokens + type workhonest gap = lacuna

catalog_v1card drawer

ep:1ep:46

Card Catalog

Buff stock, typewriter, date stamps, a rod through the drawer.

The hook: the app already calls itself this — the path bar reads ~/catalog/{id} — and the interaction is already the metaphor. A compact row expanding in place is a card lifted from a drawer and read, then dropped back.

Muted almost to monochrome by design: an index card carries one ink and one stamp colour, so the five type colours have to earn their presence rather than being handed a full spectrum.

token swapexpand-in-place = the metaphor

fieldguide_v1plate 14

ep:1ep:46

Field Guide

Specimen plates, taxonomic keys, hand-lettered annotation.

The hook: your data is already a taxonomy — kind: season / movie / ova / special is a rank, series_relations is a cladogram, and the source-scope rule is the working definition of a species boundary.

A field guide is also honest by construction: plates carry "range uncertain" and "not observed" as first-class states, drawn with the same care as a confirmed sighting. That is your empty-state brief, already illustrated.

token swap343 series = a taxonomy
04

Instrument & machine

Where the article's Industrial Control Panel lands once it is pulled toward what this app actually does — comparing two runs of the same thing and being candid about where one of them stops.

telemetry_v1two traces

ep:1ep:46

Telemetry

Mission control — synchronised traces, warning stripes, machine labels.

The hook: two runs of one story, plotted against a shared clock, diverging and rejoining. Flight telemetry is the discipline that displays exactly that, and it has a hard-won convention for missing data: loss of signal is drawn as its own state, hatched and labelled, never as a flat line pretending the signal was zero.

Your card is already called the instrument. This is what happens if you take that name completely literally.

token swapgap = loss of signal

flatbed_v1reel 3

ep:1ep:46

Flatbed

An editing bay — timeline, splice marks, leader tape, grease pencil.

The hook: the most literally true metaphor on the board. Adaptation is editing. Filler is inserted footage, a compressed arc is a cut, an anime-original ending is a rewritten reel, and volume boundaries are reel changes.

It brings a motion vocabulary with it that your print-style card entrance would sit comfortably beside: leader countdown, sprocket registration, the frame-accurate scrub. Grease-pencil marks are what a human writes directly on the film when something needs flagging — which is what a manual anchor is.

tokens + motionadaptation = editing
05

Drawn from the source

One specimen, and the only one whose visual language comes from the medium the app is about rather than from an instrument that resembles it.

tankobon_v1right to left

ep:1ep:46

Tankōbon

Ink, screentone, panel gutters, an obi band around a real object.

The hook: the medium already solved your central problem. The gutter — the white channel between panels — is where a manga puts everything it deliberately does not show, and readers understand it perfectly without being told. That is the honest gap, native.

Screentone gives you a density encoding that is greyscale by default, so filler density can read without spending a single hue; the spot colour stays reserved for the one thing that matters. And a volume is a physical object with a spine — which is the only theme here where vol:108/110 is a picture rather than a number.

tone workgutter = the honest gap
06

Rolled, not chosen

The fourth theme was not picked from this board. It was generated from a 128-character random string — which is the method surrealism actually used: the exquisite corpse, automatic drawing, Duchamp dropping three metre-long threads and keeping the curves they fell in as a new unit of measurement. Base-36 slices of the seed map to enumerated options and hue angles, so the theme is reproducible and every choice is inspectable.

The seed · 128 chars, [a-z0-9], from /dev/urandom

3oo5578m6mbjz7x62ifonq0ohcnyfwdfv0be1ymwpsfghrkx3xmdze4sx4po5d80jg6o28d3nseg901506o7uukvc0q8jsfctafcrz7c51jqksie4a5p5lqh9q8s3bz8

Chars 0–15 → the theme law: impossible stair · drift · cut paper · warped mono · void ground · complementary pair. Plus radius 4px, gutter 9px, shadow 41%, skew 2°. Chars 16–47, 48–79 and 80–111 are the three variant palettes.

What the seed does not get to decide: the lightness and saturation bands are designer-set, so a roll can pick any hue relationship it likes and never produce mud. The same split syntax_v1 already ran, where the original hues failed as-is and were validator-tuned rather than accepted.

Glasshouse

A conservatory after dark — pressed specimens, staged at depth.

Material: pressed specimen. Matte stock, deckled edges, the soft shadow of a herbarium sheet. The roll handed over greens, sand and one cold blue, and the flats read as pressed leaves.

Nudged: filler, anime and manga landed within 14° of each other. Anime moved to violet at 275°, which is also the better semantics — anime-original is the one thing in the garden that was never grown there. Now 46°.

Fairground

Poster paper with the registration a hair out of true.

Material: printed flats. Every colour edge carries a sliver of the plate beneath it. Gloss where the others are matte.

The clean roll — the only one of the three that needed no correction at all, 47° straight out of the string. Widest spread on the whole board, and the one that will stress-test whether five loud types can share an 18px strip without turning into confetti.

anaglyphchars 80–111

ep:1ep:46

Anaglyph

Every flat printed twice, red and cyan, 2° apart.

Material: channel separation. Depth you would need glasses for and are not given.

The roll broke here, and the break became the idea. It dealt mixed and anime the identical hue — 192° both — which would have made two episode types indistinguishable on the strip. Fixing it by the anaglyph's own logic put mixed on magenta at 300°, the colour red and cyan actually make where they overlap. The type that means "both" is now the colour that means "both".

What came of it

Seven specimens became variants of three themes; five were set aside; a fourth theme was rolled. The full architecture — the four layers, what a theme owns versus a variant, and all twelve variants on the real card — lives in the companion document, Theme Contract.

  1. The five set aside are not wasted. Their ideas travel even though their skins don't: lacuna from Concordance as the word for a marked, measured, never-invented gap; loss of signal from Telemetry as a state drawn in its own right rather than as a flat line; and Field Guide's insistence that "not observed" is illustrated with the same care as a confirmed sighting — which is the empty-state brief exactly.
  2. Archive was the runner-up for the fourth slot and lost it to a random string. If a fifth theme is ever wanted, it arrives with three coherent variants already assembled.
  3. Next: the Terminal Lab — live presets over the real card, real strip, real episode list, switchable in place, the way Card Skin and Keycap were settled. Terminal goes first because it is cheapest and rides rulings that already shipped, so it is the honest test of whether the theme/variant split survives contact.
  4. Phosphor is the first variant built, deliberately. One hue carrying five episode types is the likeliest failure on the board, and finding that out early is worth more than three easy wins.
  5. Then you rule, it ports, and it lands in DESIGN_SYSTEM.md with a name and a date — same as instrument_v1, interior_v1, kbd_v4.

Texture belongs to zone, not to type — permanently

The hatch in the live strip is not a type mark. It is a zone mark — 'run' | 'hatch' | 'out' — and it changes behaviour: in the hatch there are no episode numbers, so a click drives the chapter field instead of the episode field.

So the strip already encodes two independent dimensions on two separate channels: hue = type, texture + position = zone. Giving type a texture would make a hatched segment sometimes mean "I drive chapters" and sometimes "I'm filler" — the texture stops predicting what a click does.

The rule: one visual variable per dimension, and when two compete, the robust channel goes to the higher-stakes distinction. Texture survives greyscale and every dichromacy; zone changes behaviour where type only changes a label. Zone keeps texture, in every theme.

The second phosphor has to be cold

Phosphor stays monochrome by choice, so the duotone sits beside it rather than replacing it — identical chrome, palette the only variable. But which second hue is not a free choice.

Amber + P1 green scores 56%, barely better than mono, because amber↔green is the protan/deutan confusion axis — it spends a hue and buys nothing. Amber + cold blue-white scores 108%, because blue↔yellow is the axis dichromats retain. And three phosphors score 40%, worse than one, because crowding hues together lets tritanopia collapse the blue/green pair.

That generalises past this theme: any near-monochrome variant should reach for the blue-yellow axis, and adding hues is not monotonic — more is not safer.

The zone hatch itself thins at scale — set length to 1122

Switch the series to One Piece's shape: thirty-odd runs across ~1120 episodes, laid out with the real model — proportional widths, MIN_SEG_PX = 2, 1px gaps. Short runs get their 2px floor and the long ones absorb it.

The tail survives here because 38 unadapted chapters is still a wide span. But the hatch has a 6px period, so any source_only tail narrower than about 12px shows fewer than two stripes and reads as a flat dim block — the zone marker degrades into "a dimmer colour" exactly when the tail is shortest.

Worth checking against real data: a series one or two chapters ahead of its adaptation is the common case, not a rare one. If the hatch is the honest-gap marker, it should not be the thing that disappears when the gap is small.

Three of four now beat what ships

Blueprint 132%, daylight 122%, phosphor duotone 108% — all more robust than the live palette, and all reached without leaving their identity: blueprint's canon stayed locked to the cyan family, daylight's every type clears 4.5:1 on paper, the duotone kept phosphor's exact chrome.

Treat the numbers as a floor, not a finish. These came from an optimiser with no taste term, so they want the same hand pass syntax_v1 got.

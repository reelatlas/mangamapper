Theme Contract

Four themes, twelve variants, four layers. A theme owns behaviour — type, motion, interaction, and its optional graphics. A variant owns appearance, material included. Neither is ever allowed to touch the rulings underneath.

01

The four layers

Your split — theme owns graphics, animation and interaction; variant owns colour — works, but it needs a layer above it and a layer below it to be safe. Above: the graphics layer has to be separable or the toggle can't exist. Below: a large body of locked rulings must survive every theme, and the contract is what stops a theme from quietly reopening them.

Layer 0 · lockedStructure

Not themeable, by any theme, ever. These are correctness rulings, not taste — the strip's pixel-snapped geometry and clamped indicator ticks, the meta-row baseline strut, position math, the focus-ownership laws, scroll anchoring by walk direction, component-owned scroll.

A theme that needs one of these to change is rejected, not accommodated. This layer is why the contract is worth writing down: months of rulings live here and a theming pass is exactly the kind of work that erodes them by accident.

Layer 1 · per themeBehaviour

What makes a theme a world rather than a palette. Typeface and scale, the border / radius / elevation vocabulary, the motion vocabulary, the construction model, and the interaction grammar — what selection looks like, what a cursor is, how depth reads.

Built once per theme and inherited unchanged by all its variants. This is where nearly all the engineering cost sits, which is the argument for four themes rather than twelve.

The line against layer 2: this layer says what a thing is and does; layer 2 says what it is made of. "Everything is a flat that occludes and slides" is construction, so it lives here. "The paper is waxed, deckled, and slightly foxed" is material, so it doesn't.

Layer 2 · per variantAppearance

The surface ramp, the five --type-* colours, the semantic colours, and — amended, per your call — the whole of material: glow, hatch angle, tone density, texture, ink weight, edge treatment.

That amendment is what buys the cheap aesthetic wins. The theme states the role — "the title is the card's one accent" — and each variant answers it in its own material: phosphor glow, ink weight, a rule beneath, a drop shadow off a paper edge. No theme needs re-engineering to get a genuinely different look.

A variant stays cheap: a token block and nothing else. That is the test for whether something belongs here. If a variant needs new markup or new behaviour, it is a theme wearing the wrong label.

Layer 3 · toggleableAtmosphere

The graphics layer. Per theme, off by default on anything that can't prove it can carry it, and switchable by hand — persisted exactly like the minimap preference already is (useMinimapPref, localStorage, private-mode safe).

Lazy-loaded with the theme that needs it, so a terminal user never downloads a renderer.

The rule that makes the toggle honest

Graphics-off is the design target, not the degraded mode. Every theme has to be finished and good with the layer disabled — that is the state most people will see, and it is the only state you can guarantee.

Which gives one hard constraint: the atmosphere layer carries no information. Never a value, a state, or an affordance that exists only there. It sits behind and around the instrument, never inside it — nothing renders under the card interior, the strip, or the episode list, all of which have frame budgets they already spend.

Auto-off without asking: prefers-reduced-motion, coarse pointer, small viewport, and a dropped-frame bail-out. The manual toggle is for taste; those are for not shipping a slideshow.

05

paper theatre

Theme four, generated rather than designed — which is the method the movement actually used. Surrealism ran on chance operations: the exquisite corpse, automatic drawing, Duchamp dropping three metre-long threads and keeping the curves they landed in as a new unit of measurement. So the theme was rolled from a 128-character random string, and the string is in the document so the roll is reproducible and inspectable.

The roll

3oo5578m6mbjz7x62ifonq0ohcnyfwdfv0be1ymwpsfghrkx3xmdze4sx4po5d80jg6o28d3nseg901506o7uukvc0q8jsfctafcrz7c51jqksie4a5p5lqh9q8s3bz8

Chars 0–15 set the theme law (shared by every variant); chars 16–47, 48–79 and 80–111 are the three variant palettes; 112–127 are tuning constants. Base-36 slices map to enumerated options and hue angles.

What the seed does not get to decide: the lightness and saturation bands. Those are designer-set, so a roll can pick any hue relationship it likes and still never produce mud — the same split your syntax_v1 palette already ran, where the original hues "failed as-is" and were validator-tuned rather than accepted.

The guardrail this theme needs and the others don't

Your product promise is that a position shown to a user is true, and an honest gap beats a plausible guess. A surreal theme is in direct tension with that — so the rule is: the surrealism lives in the chrome, never in the values.

The stair may misbehave, the flats may drift, the type may warp. ep:1122/— stays dead literal, the strip stays pixel-snapped, and the episode list never lies for effect. A theme that makes the data feel uncertain has broken layer 0, whatever it looks like.

06

Settled & open

Two calls closed, two still live, and the facts on the library you shared.

Settled

Material moved to layer 2

Glow, hatch, tone, texture, ink weight and edge treatment now sit with colour in the variant, not with behaviour in the theme.

This is what makes cheap aesthetic wins possible: a theme states the role, each variant answers it in its own material. Construction stays at layer 1 — "everything is a flat that occludes and slides" is how a thing is built, not what it's made of, which is the line paper theatre had to test first.

Settled

Tankōbon gets seinen and scan

Both built above. Seinen proves the theme survives losing its loudest instrument; scan is the one that would make the app look found rather than designed.

Scan is also the best test of the amendment. Everything that distinguishes it — yellowing, faded ink, moiré, a rotation that never resolves — is layer 2. If it lands without a single behavioural change, the material call was right.

Open

The warped-mono type axis

Paper theatre rolled "warped mono", which wants a variable font with a real width or slant axis — the app's IBM Plex Mono has neither in the weights you load.

Three ways out: swap to a variable mono for this theme only; fake the warp with a CSS transform per glyph run; or read the roll as character rather than mechanism and pick a mono that is already strange. I'd try the third first — it costs nothing and the other two are recoverable if it looks tame.

Open

Whether the impossible stair is shippable

A back gesture that lands somewhere plausible but wrong is a wonderful surreal device and a genuinely bad navigation bug — and the two are indistinguishable from the user's side.

My instinct: keep the stair visual, not functional. The breadcrumb can depict an impossible descent while the router behaves perfectly. That preserves the guardrail above — surreal chrome, honest mechanism — and it's the only version I'd be willing to ship.

The order from here

  1. Terminal first, all three variants. Cheapest, rides rulings that already shipped, and it is the honest test of whether the split survives contact — if phosphor, blueprint and daylight really are just token blocks, the contract holds and the other three themes get much less risky.
  2. Then depth, once, with core as the target. Build the descent against the variant that needs no graphics. If it's good there, abyss's atmosphere is enrichment rather than life support — §01's rule applied as a build order.
  3. Paper theatre and tankōbon last, and in either order. Both are further from the current app than the first two, and both will benefit from a theming mechanism that has already been exercised twice.
  4. The strip is the fast test at every stage. Five distinguishable types at 18px with 1px gaps. Phosphor, tankōbon and fairground each fight it from a different direction — one hue, near-greyscale, and five loud hues at once. Run it early on those three.
  5. Then the lab — live presets over the real card, switchable in place, the way Card Skin and Keycap were settled. Then you rule, it ports, and it lands in DESIGN_SYSTEM.md with a name and a date.
